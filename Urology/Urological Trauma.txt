A 28-year-old man is brought to the ER after a motorcycle accident with a deceleration mechanism. He has flank ecchymosis and gross hematuria. What is the single best indicator of significant renal injury in this patient?
A) Presence of hematuria
B) Degree of flank ecchymosis
C) Rib fracture
D) Mechanism of injury alone
E) Blood pressure on arrival
Correct Answer: A) Presence of hematuria
Explanation: Hematuria is the best indicator of significant renal injury, though its degree does not correlate reliably with injury severity, so imaging decisions are not based on hematuria grade alone.
### 
A 40-year-old man sustains a stab wound to the flank. Compared to blunt renal trauma, penetrating renal trauma is characterized by:
A) Lower rate of complications
B) Higher percentage of complications and need for surgical intervention
C) Lower likelihood of requiring surgical intervention
D) Absence of hematuria
E) Predominantly reno-vascular injury from deceleration
Correct Answer: B) Higher percentage of complications and need for surgical intervention
Explanation: Penetrating trauma (gunshot/stab) causes a higher rate of complications and more often requires surgical intervention than blunt trauma.
### 
Which finding is most specifically associated with reno-vascular (pedicle) injury after blunt trauma?
A) Rib fracture
B) Flank tenderness
C) Significant acceleration/deceleration mechanism
D) Low-velocity mechanism
E) Isolated microscopic hematuria
Correct Answer: C) Significant acceleration/deceleration mechanism
Explanation: Significant deceleration causes tearing at points of fixation such as the renal hilum, making this the key historical clue for vascular injury.
### 
A hemodynamically stable adult sustains blunt abdominal trauma with one documented episode of hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg) and microscopic hematuria. Renal imaging is indicated because:
A) Microscopic hematuria with an episode of hypotension is an indication for imaging
B) All blunt trauma requires imaging regardless of findings
C) Imaging is never needed if hematuria is microscopic
D) Only gross hematuria warrants imaging
E) Imaging is reserved for penetrating trauma only
Correct Answer: A) Microscopic hematuria with an episode of hypotension is an indication for imaging
Explanation: Blunt trauma with microscopic hematuria plus even one episode of hypotension (SBP <90) is a specific indication for renal imaging, unlike simple stable microscopic hematuria.
### 
The gold standard imaging modality for suspected renal trauma is:
A) Renal ultrasound
B) MRI
C) Intravenous pyelogram
D) Contrast-enhanced CT scan
E) Plain KUB radiograph
Correct Answer: D) Contrast-enhanced CT scan
Explanation: Contrast-enhanced CT is the gold standard for renal trauma, allowing grading of injury and assessment of associated structures (hilum, perinephric space, vena cava).
### 
One-shot intraoperative IVP performed during renal trauma surgery is primarily used to:
A) Grade the degree of renal laceration
B) Diagnose bladder rupture
C) Assess function of the contralateral kidney
D) Detect ureteral stones
E) Replace CT imaging entirely
Correct Answer: C) Assess function of the contralateral kidney
Explanation: The one-shot IVP (10 minutes after IV contrast push) is mainly used intraoperatively to confirm that the contralateral kidney is functioning before proceeding with nephrectomy if needed.
### 
Which of the following is the primary criterion guiding management of renal trauma?
A) Grade of injury on CT
B) Hemodynamic stability
C) Presence of gross vs microscopic hematuria
D) Patient age
E) Mechanism (blunt vs penetrating)
Correct Answer: B) Hemodynamic stability
Explanation: Hemodynamic stability is the primary criterion determining management; stable patients are typically managed conservatively regardless of imaging grade, unstable patients require intervention.
### 
Selective angio-embolization is most indicated in renal trauma when CT shows:
A) Contrast leak, AV fistula, or aneurysm
B) Small subcapsular hematoma only
C) Isolated cortical laceration without bleeding
D) Normal contralateral kidney
E) Perinephric fat stranding only
Correct Answer: A) Contrast leak, AV fistula, or aneurysm
Explanation: CT findings of active contrast extravasation, arteriovenous fistula, or aneurysm are indications for selective angio-embolization.
### 
Which of the following is an indication for renal exploration after trauma?
A) Stable microscopic hematuria
B) Grade 1 renal contusion
C) Expanding or pulsatile perirenal hematoma found at laparotomy
D) Stable patient with contrast leak amenable to embolization
E) Isolated flank ecchymosis without hematuria
Correct Answer: C) Expanding or pulsatile perirenal hematoma found at laparotomy
Explanation: An expanding or pulsatile perirenal hematoma discovered at laparotomy, along with non-response to resuscitation, grade 5 vascular injury, or failed embolization, are indications for surgical exploration.
### 
A patient with pelvic fracture and gross hematuria is suspected of having a bladder injury. Which imaging study should be performed to exclude bladder rupture?
A) Renal ultrasound
B) Retrograde urethrogram
C) Non-contrast CT KUB
D) Cystography
E) MRI pelvis
Correct Answer: D) Cystography
Explanation: Any patient with pelvic fracture and gross (visible) hematuria should undergo cystography to exclude bladder rupture, as visible hematuria is the principal sign of bladder trauma.
### 
Extraperitoneal bladder rupture most commonly results from:
A) A sudden blow to a distended bladder
B) Pelvic fracture
C) Iatrogenic catheter trauma
D) Penetrating abdominal injury
E) Spontaneous rupture in a healthy bladder
Correct Answer: B) Pelvic fracture
Explanation: Extraperitoneal bladder injury is almost always secondary to pelvic fracture, whereas intraperitoneal rupture typically results from a blow to a distended bladder raising intravesical pressure.
### 
On cystography, intraperitoneal bladder rupture is classically identified by:
A) Contrast leak outlining bowel loops
B) Flame-shaped extravasation in perivesical soft tissue
C) No extravasation at all
D) Contrast pooling only within the bladder lumen
E) Extravasation limited to the prostatic urethra
Correct Answer: A) Contrast leak outlining bowel loops
Explanation: Intraperitoneal rupture shows contrast outlining bowel loops as it leaks into the peritoneal cavity, while extraperitoneal rupture shows a flame-shaped perivesical extravasation pattern.
### 
Management of an isolated extraperitoneal bladder rupture without bladder neck involvement is:
A) Immediate surgical repair
B) Suprapubic cystostomy only, no catheter
C) Conservative management with Foley catheter drainage
D) Percutaneous nephrostomy
E) Cystectomy
Correct Answer: C) Conservative management with Foley catheter drainage
Explanation: Uncomplicated extraperitoneal bladder rupture is managed conservatively with Foley catheter drainage; surgery is reserved for bladder neck involvement or associated injuries. Intraperitoneal rupture, in contrast, always requires surgical repair.
### 
A man reports a popping sound during vigorous intercourse followed by immediate detumescence, pain, and eggplant-shaped swelling of the penis. Blood is noted at the urethral meatus. The most appropriate next step is:
A) Immediate surgical exploration without further workup
B) CT scan of the pelvis
C) Reassurance and outpatient follow-up
D) Retrograde urethrography before catheterization
E) Blind Foley catheter placement to relieve retention
Correct Answer: D) Retrograde urethrography before catheterization
Explanation: Blood at the urethral meatus after suspected penile fracture indicates possible urethral injury; a retrograde urethrogram must be performed before attempting catheterization.
### 
In penile fracture, the structure that must be surgically repaired is the:
A) Tunica albuginea
B) Buck’s fascia
C) Dartos fascia
D) Corpus spongiosum only
E) Skin alone
Correct Answer: A) Tunica albuginea
Explanation: Penile fracture is a tear of the tunica albuginea; surgical exploration, hematoma evacuation, and closure of this tear is the definitive management and reduces complications compared with conservative treatment.
###
A 58-year-old male smoker presents with one episode of microscopic hematuria. What is the next best step in management?
A) CT abdomen and pelvis without contrast
B) Renal ultrasound
C) CT urography with delayed (excretory) phase
D) Repeat urinalysis after 3 months
E) MRI abdomen and pelvis
Correct Answer: C) CT urography with delayed (excretory) phase
Explanation:
In an older smoker (high malignancy risk), microscopic hematuria warrants full upper tract evaluation with CT urography including the delayed excretory phase to assess the collecting system, plus cystoscopy — a non-contrast study or ultrasound alone would miss urothelial lesions and collecting-system pathology.
