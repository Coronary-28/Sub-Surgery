Regarding urological imaging studies, what is the most important risk of exposure to ionizing radiation?
A) Alopecia
B) Diarrhoea
C) Induction of malignancy
D) Skin irritation
E) Cataracts
Correct Answer: C) Induction of malignancy
Explanation: Ionizing radiation from CT and fluoroscopic urological studies carries a cumulative risk of carcinogenesis, making malignancy induction the most clinically significant long-term hazard. Skin irritation and cataracts require far higher cumulative doses to manifest.
###
All of the following are true about kidney anatomy, EXCEPT:
A) An oval intraperitoneal organ
B) Left kidney has a longer renal vein
C) Right kidney is smaller than the left kidney
D) Kidneys are bean-shaped structures
E) Kidneys are bilateral structures
Correct Answer: A) An oval intraperitoneal organ
Explanation: Kidneys are retroperitoneal, bean-shaped structures, not intraperitoneal. The right kidney sits lower and is often described as slightly smaller, while the left renal vein is longer as it crosses the aorta to reach the IVC.
###
Which of the following is FALSE about kidney anatomy?
A) Kidneys are retroperitoneal organs
B) Kidneys are bilateral
C) Kidneys are bean-shaped
D) Kidneys are intraperitoneal organs
E) Kidneys lie posterior in the abdomen
Correct Answer: D) Kidneys are intraperitoneal organs
Explanation: Kidneys lie retroperitoneally against the posterior abdominal wall, not within the peritoneal cavity. All other listed features (bilateral, bean-shaped, posterior location) accurately describe renal anatomy.
###
The correct order of the division of the intrarenal branches of the renal artery is:
A) Segmental, interlobar (infundibular), arcuate, interlobular
B) Segmental, arcuate, interlobular, interlobar (infundibular)
C) Interlobular, segmental, interlobar (infundibular), arcuate
D) Segmental, arcuate, interlobar (infundibular), interlobular
E) Interlobular, interlobar (infundibular), segmental, arcuate
Correct Answer: A) Segmental, interlobar (infundibular), arcuate, interlobular
Explanation: The renal artery branches sequentially from segmental arteries into interlobar arteries running between pyramids, then arcuate arteries arching at the corticomedullary junction, and finally interlobular arteries ascending into the cortex.
###
The only segment of the urethra that does NOT have transitional epithelium is the:
A) Prostatic urethra
B) Membranous urethra
C) Bulbar urethra
D) Bulbomembranous urethra
E) Fossa navicularis
Correct Answer: E) Fossa navicularis
Explanation: The fossa navicularis is lined by stratified squamous epithelium, distinguishing it from the transitional epithelium lining the more proximal urethral segments listed as options. Another way to phrase the question: Which segment is lined by stratified squamous epithelium? Ans: E.
###
Ultrasound has many advantages which include all of the following EXCEPT:

B) It requires little or no pre-procedural preparation
C) It allows discrimination between pseudotumors and true lesions

Which of the following is associated with suprapubic pain?
A) Renal colic
B) Prostatic cancer

D) Ureteric stone

Correct Answer: C) Bladder disease

Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding physical examination?

C) The normal prostate is the size of a walnut

Correct Answer: D) A hydrocele feels like a bag of worms
Explanation: A "bag of worms" texture describes a varicocele, not a hydrocele; a hydrocele is a smooth, fluid-filled swelling that transilluminates. A warm room helps relax the cremasteric reflex for accurate examination.
###
All the following can cause a DECREASE in specific gravity of urine EXCEPT:
A) Dehydration
B) Increased fluid intake
C) Diuretics
D) Inability to concentrate urine
E) Diabetes insipidus
Correct Answer: A) Dehydration
Explanation: Dehydration concentrates the urine, raising specific gravity, whereas increased fluid intake, diuretics, and impaired concentrating ability all dilute urine and lower specific gravity.

B) Urea, glucose, and sodium
C) Glucose, sodium, and potassium
D) Sodium, chloride, and urea

###
Which of the following is true about renal pain?
A) Caused by acute distension of renal capsule from obstruction or inflammation
B) Inflammation is steady in nature
C) Can be confused with intraperitoneal diseases
D) All of the above
E) None of the above
Correct Answer: D) All of the above
Explanation: Renal pain arises from capsular distension, with inflammatory pain being steady while obstructive pain fluctuates; it can also mimic intraperitoneal pathology, though patients with renal pain classically prefer to move (rolling sign) unlike those with peritoneal pain.

###
Which of the following drugs does NOT cause renal failure?
A) ACE inhibitor
B) Gentamicin
C) Tamsulosin
D) NSAIDs
E) Aminoglycosides
Correct Answer: C) Tamsulosin
Explanation: Tamsulosin is an alpha-blocker used for bladder outlet obstruction and lacks nephrotoxic potential, unlike ACE inhibitors, aminoglycosides such as gentamicin, and NSAIDs, which can impair renal perfusion or cause direct tubular injury.
###
Which imaging modality is safest for a patient in renal failure?
A) CT with contrast
B) MRI with gadolinium
C) Ultrasound
D) IVU
E) CT urography
Correct Answer: C) Ultrasound
Explanation: Ultrasound requires no contrast or radiation exposure and is unaffected by renal function, making it the safest imaging choice in renal impairment, unlike iodinated or gadolinium-based contrast studies which risk nephrotoxicity or nephrogenic systemic fibrosis.
###
Renal pain classically radiates to which of the following?
A) Shoulder
B) Flank to groin
C) Neck
D) Lower abdomen only
E) Contralateral flank
Correct Answer: B) Flank to groin
Explanation: Renal and ureteric pain radiates from the flank toward the groin and may be referred to the testis due to shared T11-T12 innervation, distinguishing it from intraperitoneal pain, which classically radiates to the shoulder.
###
Which of the following is the correct sequence of the male urethra from proximal to distal?
A) Membranous, prostatic, bulbar, penile
B) Prostatic, membranous, penile, bulbar
C) Prostatic, bulbar, membranous, penile
D) Bulbar, prostatic, membranous, penile
E) Prostatic, membranous, bulbar, penile
Correct Answer: E) Prostatic, membranous, bulbar, penile
Explanation: The male urethra begins at the bladder neck, passing through the prostatic segment, then the membranous urethra at the external sphincter, followed by the bulbar and pendulous penile urethra ending at the fossa navicularis.
###
A patient presents with flank pain. Which feature would favor a urological (renal) rather than an intraperitoneal cause?
A) Pain is steady and patient lies motionless
B) Pain radiates to the shoulder
C) Patient prefers to move around and roll to find comfort
D) Pain is aggravated by movement and relieved by lying still
E) Pain is associated with peritoneal signs
Correct Answer: C) Patient prefers to move around and roll to find comfort
Explanation: Renal colic patients characteristically move restlessly (rolling sign) due to colicky pain, whereas intraperitoneal pathology causes steady pain with patients preferring to remain motionless.
###
Pneumaturia is best defined as which of the following?
A) Painful urination due to inflammation
B) Presence of gas in the urine
C) Involuntary loss of urine during sleep
D) Presence of blood in the urine
E) Presence of protein in the urine
Correct Answer: B) Presence of gas in the urine
Explanation: Pneumaturia results from recent catheterization, an enteric fistula, or infection with gas-forming organisms, distinguishing it from hematuria (blood) or enuresis (nocturnal incontinence).

B) It secretes fructose
C) It secretes albumin
D) It secretes the majority of seminal fluid volume
E) It secretes testosterone
Correct Answer: A) It secretes citric acid

###
In a 30-year-old patient with microscopic hematuria confirmed on two consecutive urinalyses, which combination correctly represents the essential investigations and the causes of hematuria to consider?
A) Essential investigations: cystoscopy and upper tract imaging; Causes: urinary tract stones, urothelial/renal malignancy, glomerular disease
B) Essential investigations: urine culture and complete blood count; Causes: dehydration, excessive fluid intake, and diuretic use
C) Essential investigations: serum PSA and testosterone level; Causes: hydrocele, varicocele, and epididymitis
D) Essential investigations: 24-hour urine collection and renal biopsy in all cases; Causes: nocturnal polyuria, urgency, and frequency
E) Essential investigations: uroflowmetry and post-void residual; Causes: bladder outlet obstruction, prostatic enlargement, and urethral stricture
Correct Answer: A) Essential investigations: cystoscopy and upper tract imaging; Causes: urinary tract stones, urothelial/renal malignancy, glomerular disease
Explanation: Hematuria in an adult is malignancy until proven otherwise, so cystoscopy assesses the bladder while upper tract imaging evaluates the kidneys and ureters, with stones, malignancy, and glomerular disease as the key causes to exclude. The distractors instead describe irritative symptom workup, scrotal pathology, or bladder outlet obstruction assessment, none of which target hematuria evaluation.
###
All of the following are appropriate questions to ask when taking a history of hematuria EXCEPT:

Correct Answer: E) Does the patient experience morning stiffness lasting more than 30 minutes?

###
All of the following are irritative LUTS symptoms EXCEPT:
A) Frequency
B) Urgency
C) Nocturia
D) Dysuria
E) Hesitancy
Correct Answer: E) Hesitancy
Explanation: Hesitancy is an obstructive symptom reflecting delayed initiation of urination, whereas frequency, urgency, nocturia, and dysuria are all irritative symptoms related to bladder storage and mucosal irritation.
###
A 68-year-old man reports voiding 10 times during the day and waking up 3 times at night to urinate, each time passing only a small volume, with a strong sudden desire to void that is difficult to defer. His total 24-hour urine output is normal. Which category of symptoms best describes this presentation?
A) Obstructive symptoms
B) Irritative symptoms
C) Continuous incontinence
D) Overflow incontinence
E) Nocturnal polyuria
Correct Answer: B) Irritative symptoms
Explanation: Frequency, nocturia with small voided volumes, and urgency despite a normal total daily urine output point to decreased functional bladder capacity or mucosal irritation, characteristic of irritative rather than obstructive symptoms.
