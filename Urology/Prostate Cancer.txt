Which statement about carcinoma of the prostate is correct?
A) It is typically a squamous cell carcinoma
B) It is graded histologically using the Gleason score
C) It is not influenced by hormone manipulation
D) It commonly invades the rectum directly
E) It occurs predominantly in the central zone
Correct Answer: B) It is graded histologically using the Gleason score
Explanation: Prostate cancer is an adenocarcinoma graded by the Gleason score, arises predominantly in the peripheral zone, is hormone-dependent (responds to androgen deprivation therapy), and rarely invades the rectum due to Denonvilliers' fascia.
###
What grading system is used for prostate cancer?
A) Gleason score
B) Bloom-Richardson
C) Breslow thickness
D) Fuhrman grade
E) Ann Arbor staging
Correct Answer: A) Gleason score
Explanation: The Gleason score assesses glandular architecture on low-power microscopy and correlates with prognosis; Bloom-Richardson is used for breast cancer, Breslow for melanoma, and Fuhrman for renal cell carcinoma.
###
PSA density is calculated as:
A) Free PSA divided by total PSA
B) Total PSA multiplied by prostate volume
C) Free PSA divided by prostate volume
D) Total PSA divided by total prostate volume
E) Total PSA divided by patient age
Correct Answer: D) Total PSA divided by total prostate volume
Explanation: PSA density is total serum PSA divided by prostate volume (measured by TRUS); values above 0.15 ng/mL/g raise suspicion for malignancy.
###
Serum PSA and digital rectal examination are primarily used for:
A) Treatment planning after diagnosis
B) Early detection/screening for prostate cancer
C) Assessing bladder outflow obstruction
D) Diagnosing urinary tract infection
Correct Answer: B) Early detection/screening for prostate cancer
Explanation: PSA testing combined with DRE is used for early detection of prostate cancer in asymptomatic men, though PSA is organ-specific rather than cancer-specific.
###
Watchful waiting (as opposed to active surveillance) is most appropriate for men who:
A) Have no major comorbidities
B) Have PSA less than 10 ng/mL
C) Have a life expectancy of 10 years or less with well-to-moderately differentiated cancer
D) Have impalpable cancer not visible on imaging
E) Are exactly 70 years old
Correct Answer: C) Have a life expectancy of 10 years or less with well-to-moderately differentiated cancer
Explanation: Watchful waiting is a palliative, symptom-directed strategy reserved for patients with limited life expectancy (≤10 years) and less aggressive disease, distinct from active surveillance which is used in younger, low-risk patients with curative intent.
###
The most common site of prostate cancer metastasis is:
A) Lumbar spine/vertebrae
B) Liver
C) Lung
Correct Answer: A) Lumbar spine/vertebrae
Explanation: Prostate cancer classically metastasizes to the axial skeleton, particularly the lumbar spine, producing osteoblastic (sclerotic) lesions with associated back pain and elevated alkaline phosphatase.
###
Which statement about the Gleason grading system is FALSE?
A) It is commonly used for prostate cancer
B) It correlates with prognosis
C) It does not correlate with prognosis
D) A higher score indicates worse prognosis
Correct Answer: C) It does not correlate with prognosis
Explanation: This statement is false — the Gleason score strongly correlates with prognosis and guides management decisions in prostate cancer.
###
Serum PSA elevation may occur in association with which of the following?
A) Prostate enlargement (BPH)
B) All of the above
C) Prostatic inflammation
D) Prostate cancer
E) Prostate manipulation
Correct Answer: B) All of the above
Explanation: PSA is organ-specific, not disease-specific, and can be elevated by BPH, prostatitis, cancer, and mechanical manipulation of the prostate (e.g., catheterization, biopsy, DRE).
###
Which is the most common cancer diagnosed in urology?
A) Prostate cancer
B) Bladder cancer
C) Renal cell carcinoma
D) Testicular cancer
Correct Answer: A) Prostate cancer
Explanation: Prostate cancer is the second most common cancer diagnosed in men overall and the most common urological malignancy.
###
A patient with prostate cancer has a Gleason score of 8 and PSA 25. Which of the following is NOT an appropriate management option?
A) Radical prostatectomy
B) Radiotherapy
C) Active surveillance
Correct Answer: C) Active surveillance
Explanation: Active surveillance is reserved for low-risk disease; a Gleason score of 8 with PSA 25 indicates high-risk disease requiring definitive treatment such as surgery or radiotherapy with hormonal therapy.
###
A patient with Gleason 3+3 prostate cancer is best managed with:
A) Radical prostatectomy
B) Radiotherapy
C) Active surveillance
Correct Answer: C) Active surveillance
Explanation: Gleason 3+3 (grade group 1) represents low-risk disease, for which active surveillance is generally preferred to avoid overtreatment, provided other risk factors (PSA, stage, patient preference) support this approach.
###
A patient has a suspicious prostatic mass on digital rectal exam with PSA of 9 ng/mL. The next step is:
A) TRUS alone without biopsy
B) Active surveillance
C) Image-guided prostate biopsy
D) Watchful waiting
Correct Answer: C) Image-guided prostate biopsy
Explanation: A suspicious DRE finding combined with elevated PSA warrants histological diagnosis via ultrasound-guided prostate biopsy rather than proceeding directly to surveillance or watchful waiting.
###
All of the following are indications for prostate biopsy EXCEPT:
A) Hard, nodular mass on DRE
B) Serum PSA of 20 ng/mL
C) PSA free:total ratio of 30%
D) PSA density of 0.28 ng/mL/cc
Correct Answer: C) PSA free:total ratio of 30%
Explanation: A high free:total PSA ratio (such as 30%) is reassuring and associated with a lower likelihood of cancer; a low ratio would instead raise suspicion and support biopsy.
###
The Gleason score is used specifically for grading:
A) Prostate cancer
B) BPH
C) Kidney stones
D) Bladder cancer staging
Correct Answer: A) Prostate cancer
Explanation: The Gleason score is a histological grading system exclusively for prostate adenocarcinoma, based on glandular differentiation patterns.
###
A patient with known advanced prostate cancer presents with new-onset back pain. The most appropriate immediate step is:
A) Watchful waiting
B) Radical prostatectomy
C) Radiotherapy alone
D) Urgent assessment for spinal metastasis/cord compression, then consideration of androgen-deprivation therapy
Correct Answer: D) Urgent assessment for spinal metastasis/cord compression, then consideration of androgen-deprivation therapy
Explanation: New back pain in a patient with known prostate cancer must first be evaluated for vertebral metastasis or spinal cord compression as an oncologic emergency; systemic androgen-deprivation therapy (GnRH/LHRH agonist) is appropriate for the underlying metastatic disease once red flags are excluded.
###
A patient with prostate cancer has Gleason grade 8 and PSA greater than 20 with no metastatic findings on staging. Which management option is NOT appropriate?
A) Active surveillance
B) Radical prostatectomy
C) Radiotherapy and hormonal therapy
Correct Answer: A) Active surveillance
Explanation: Active surveillance is unsuitable for high-risk, non-metastatic disease (Gleason 8, PSA >20); definitive treatment with surgery or radiotherapy plus hormonal therapy is indicated.
###
After one year of therapy with a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor, PSA typically:
A) Doubles
B) Decreases by approximately 50%
C) Remains unchanged
D) Increases by 50%
Correct Answer: B) Decreases by approximately 50%
Explanation: 5-alpha reductase inhibitors reduce PSA levels by approximately 50% after 6–12 months of therapy; this must be accounted for when interpreting PSA values in patients on these drugs.
###
A male patient presents to the urology department with a PSA level of 8–9 ng/mL. Digital rectal examination (DRE) reveals a hard, suspicious nodule in the prostate. What is the best next step?
A) Repeat PSA after 3 months and observe
B) Perform prostate biopsy
C) Start treatment for benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)
D) Perform a multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) of the prostate
E) Proceed directly to radical prostatectomy
Correct Answer: D) Perform a multiparametric MRI (mpMRI) of the prostate
Explanation:
With an elevated PSA and a suspicious DRE finding, mpMRI is used to localize and risk-stratify lesions before biopsy, improving biopsy targeting; jumping directly to biopsy or definitive surgery skips this standard risk-stratification step.

###
All of the following statements regarding prostate cancer are true EXCEPT:
A) Adenocarcinoma is the most common histologic type.
B) Gleason score measures tumor size.
C) Bone metastases are commonly osteoblastic.
D) PSA is used in the assessment of prostate cancer.

The Gleason score reflects histologic grade (degree of glandular differentiation), not tumor size or extent — tumor size/spread is captured by the TNM staging system instead.
