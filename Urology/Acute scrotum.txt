Which of the following diagnoses must be excluded first in a teenage boy presenting with acute left-sided testicular pain?
A) Testicular torsion
B) Epididymitis
C) Orchitis
D) Torsion of the testicular appendage
E) Testicular tumor
Correct Answer: A) Testicular torsion
Explanation:
Testicular torsion is a true surgical emergency — testicular salvage falls sharply after 6 hours, so it must always be excluded first in any acute scrotum presentation.
###
Regarding varicocele in young males, which of the following is true?
A) More common on the left side
B) Equal bilateral distribution
C) Associated with renal malignancy
D) More common on the right side
Correct Answer: A) More common on the left side
Explanation:
The left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein at a right angle and is compressed by the "nutcracker effect" (aorta and SMA), causing higher venous pressure and predisposing to left-sided varicocele.
###

A) Epididymitis
B) Orchitis
C) Torsion of the epididymal appendage
D) Testicular tumor
E) Testicular torsion
Correct Answer: A) Epididymitis
Explanation:
An older man with dysuria (UTI symptoms) and a tender, swollen testis fits epididymitis, typically caused by E. coli and other Gram-negative bacilli in this age group.

What is the sonographic hallmark of testicular torsion?

B) Epididymal edema
C) Absence of intratesticular blood flow
D) The 'blue dot' sign
E) Paratesticular fluid
Correct Answer: C) Absence of intratesticular blood flow

###
What is the definitive treatment of testicular torsion?
A) Immediate surgical exploration
B) Antibiotics
C) Observation
D) Analgesics only
Correct Answer: A) Immediate surgical exploration
Explanation:
Torsion requires urgent surgical detorsion and orchidopexy (with contralateral fixation), since testicular salvage is entirely time-dependent.
###
Which of the following is NOT a typical feature of testicular torsion?
A) High-riding testis
B) Absent cremasteric reflex
C) Nausea and vomiting
D) Urethral discharge
E) Sudden severe scrotal pain
Correct Answer: D) Urethral discharge
Explanation:
Urethral discharge suggests urethritis or an STI-related epididymo-orchitis, not torsion; torsion classically presents with sudden pain, a high-riding testis, and an absent cremasteric reflex.
###
Which of the following is NOT a sign of spermatic cord torsion?
A) Positive Prehn sign
B) Transverse lie of testis
C) Absent cremasteric reflex
D) Nausea and vomiting
Correct Answer: A) Positive Prehn sign
Explanation:
Prehn's sign (pain relief with testicular elevation) is positive in epididymitis, not torsion — in torsion, elevation does not relieve pain.
###
Which of the following statements about epididymo-orchitis is true?
A) Pain increases when the scrotum is elevated
B) It is commonly caused by E. coli in sexually active young men
C) It is typically caused by Chlamydia in a 6-year-old boy
D) Recurrent epididymo-orchitis may result from a congenital cause
Correct Answer: D) Recurrent epididymo-orchitis may result from a congenital cause
Explanation:
Recurrent epididymo-orchitis can result from an underlying congenital abnormality; Prehn's sign (pain relief, not increase, with elevation) favors epididymitis, and Chlamydia/gonorrhea (not E. coli) are typical in sexually active young men, while E. coli predominates in prepubertal boys and older men.

A man presents with a palpable right-sided varicocele of 3 months' onset, with no varicocele on the left side. Testis longitudinal axis is 5 cm bilaterally, and semen analysis reveals volume 2.0 mL, density 33 million/mL, and motility 38%. What is the next step?
A) Surgery
B) Detailed sperm morphology analysis
C) Renal ultrasound
D) CT scan
Correct Answer: C) Renal ultrasound
Explanation:
An isolated right-sided varicocele is atypical and raises suspicion for a retroperitoneal mass (e.g., RCC) compressing the right testicular vein before it enters the IVC — renal ultrasound is the appropriate initial screening step.
###
A case of an isolated right-sided varicocele. What is the next step?

Correct Answer: C) CT abdomen
Explanation:
Renal ultrasound would be the preferred first step if listed; here, CT abdomen is chosen since scrotal ultrasound only confirms the varicocele itself and cannot assess for an underlying retroperitoneal obstructing mass.

Why is varicocele more commonly left-sided than right-sided?
A) The left testicular artery is longer than the right
B) The left testicular vein enters the left renal vein at a right angle, with added compression from the nutcracker effect
C) The right testicular vein has a valve that prevents reflux
D) The left testis has a higher metabolic rate requiring more venous drainage
Correct Answer: B) The left testicular vein enters the left renal vein at a right angle, with added compression from the nutcracker effect
Explanation:
The left testicular vein drains into the left renal vein at a right angle and is compressed between the aorta and superior mesenteric artery (nutcracker effect), raising hydrostatic pressure and causing dilation and tortuosity of the pampiniform plexus; the right testicular vein drains directly into the IVC at an oblique angle.
