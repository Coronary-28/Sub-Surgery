Which of the following statements is TRUE regarding erectile dysfunction?
A) PDE-5 inhibitors can be given safely together with nitrate medications
B) The dorsal penile/pudendal nerves carry efferent (motor) fibers arising from S2–S4
C) Afferent sensory information travels from the penile ischiocavernosus/bulbocavernosus muscles to Onuf's nucleus
D) A man with preserved nocturnal erections who cannot achieve erection for intercourse is likely to have a psychogenic cause
E) There is no role for checking serum glucose and lipid profile in the evaluation of ED
Correct Answer: D) A man with preserved nocturnal erections who cannot achieve erection for intercourse is likely to have a psychogenic cause
Explanation:
Preserved nocturnal penile tumescence indicates intact vascular and neurological pathways, so failure only during intercourse points to a psychogenic cause; B and C reverse the true afferent/efferent relationships, and A and E are simply false.
###
Tumescence (the erectile phase) is mediated by:
A) Pudendal efferent fibers
B) Sacral sympathetic fibers
C) Thoracolumbar sympathetic fibers
D) Pudendal afferent fibers
E) Sacral parasympathetic fibers (S2–S4)
Correct Answer: E) Sacral parasympathetic fibers (S2–S4)
Explanation:
Parasympathetic fibers from S2–S4 release nitric oxide, which raises cGMP and relaxes cavernosal smooth muscle, producing tumescence; sympathetic (thoracolumbar) outflow instead causes detumescence.
###
All of the following are possible causes of non-ischemic (high-flow) priapism EXCEPT:
A) Iatrogenic injury
B) Blunt perineal trauma
C) Congenital arteriovenous malformation
D) Needle injury to the cavernosal artery
E) Neurologic shock
Correct Answer: E) Neurologic shock
Explanation:
Non-ischemic priapism results from an arterio-sinusoidal fistula, classically from trauma, needle injury, or iatrogenic penile surgery; neurologic shock is instead a recognized cause of ischemic (low-flow) priapism.

Regarding priapism, which of the following statements is correct?

B) It is only seen following intracavernosal injections
C) It is best diagnosed by a plain lateral X-ray of the pelvis

Correct Answer: A) It results in impotence if left untreated beyond a critical duration of prolonged ischemia

During a rigid erection, all of the following are true EXCEPT:

B) Dilation of the arterioles and arteries

D) Subtunical venous compression reducing venous outflow
E) Corporal pressure rises to several hundred millimeters of mercury

B) Phentolamine irrigation
C) Embolization
D) Penile implantation
Correct Answer: C) Embolization
Explanation:
A near-normal blood gas indicates non-ischemic (high-flow) priapism, which is managed conservatively and, if intervention is required, by selective arterial embolization rather than aspiration or agents used for ischemic priapism.

Explanation:
Ischemic (low-flow) priapism is characterized by absent or markedly reduced arterial inflow to the cavernosal artery on Doppler, with a painful, fully rigid erection.

What is the mechanism of action of PDE5 inhibitors in the treatment of erectile dysfunction?
A) They increase cAMP by inhibiting adenylate cyclase breakdown
B) They directly stimulate nitric oxide synthase
C) They prevent breakdown of cGMP, prolonging cavernosal smooth muscle relaxation
