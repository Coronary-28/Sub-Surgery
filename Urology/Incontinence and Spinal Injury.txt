Which of the following best defines stress urinary incontinence?
A) Involuntary leakage preceded by a sudden urge to void
B) Continuous leakage of urine unrelated to activity
C) Leakage of urine that occurs only during sleep
D) Leakage of urine following bladder overdistension
E) Involuntary leakage of urine associated with effort, exertion, coughing, or sneezing
Correct Answer: E) Involuntary leakage of urine associated with effort, exertion, coughing, or sneezing
Explanation: Stress incontinence occurs when rises in intra-abdominal pressure overcome urethral resistance, typically from pelvic floor weakness or sphincteric incompetence.

A patient with overactive bladder and urgency incontinence is being started on treatment. Which type of incontinence is most often managed pharmacologically as first-line therapy?
A) Stress incontinence
B) Overflow incontinence
C) Urge incontinence
D) Continuous incontinence
E) Giggle incontinence
Correct Answer: C) Urge incontinence
Explanation: Urge incontinence from detrusor overactivity responds well to antimuscarinics or beta-3 agonists (mirabegron), unlike stress incontinence, which is managed primarily with conservative or surgical measures.
###
A 48-year-old woman with no significant past medical history presents with urine leakage on coughing and sneezing. What is the most appropriate initial management?
A) Tension-free vaginal tape
B) Pelvic floor muscle training
C) Anticholinergic therapy
D) Artificial urinary sphincter
E) Retropubic suspension (Burch procedure)
Correct Answer: B) Pelvic floor muscle training
Explanation: Conservative pelvic floor exercises (at least 8 contractions, 3 times daily for 3 months) are first-line before considering surgical options such as slings or Burch colposuspension.
###
A 43-year-old woman develops constant urinary leakage 7 days after hysterectomy, not aggravated by coughing and without associated urgency. What is the most likely underlying cause?
A) Detrusor overactivity
B) Stress urinary incontinence
C) Urethral hypermobility
D) Vesicovaginal fistula
E) Urinary tract infection
Correct Answer: D) Vesicovaginal fistula
Explanation: Continuous, position-independent leakage after pelvic surgery strongly suggests an iatrogenic vesicovaginal fistula creating a direct communication between bladder and vagina.
###
Continuous incontinence, defined as leakage of urine at all times and in all positions, is most commonly caused by which of the following?
A) Detrusor overactivity
B) Bladder outlet obstruction
C) Vesicovaginal fistula
D) Intrinsic sphincter deficiency
E) Low bladder compliance
Correct Answer: C) Vesicovaginal fistula
Explanation: A fistulous communication between the bladder and vagina bypasses the normal continence mechanism entirely, producing constant leakage regardless of posture or activity.
###
In female patients, an ectopic ureter classically causes continuous urinary incontinence because the ureter inserts:
A) Distal to the external urethral sphincter (e.g., vagina or vestibule)
B) Directly into the bladder trigone
C) Proximal to the bladder neck
D) Into the ipsilateral fallopian tube
Correct Answer: A) Distal to the external urethral sphincter (e.g., vagina or vestibule)
Explanation: In females, an ectopic ureter frequently opens below the external sphincter, bypassing continence mechanisms; in males it opens above the sphincter and instead causes recurrent UTIs rather than incontinence.
###
Which of the following is NOT considered a risk factor for urinary incontinence?
A) Increasing age
B) Male sex
C) Smoking
D) Elevated BMI
E) Fecal incontinence
Correct Answer: B) Male sex
Explanation: Female sex, not male sex, is a recognized risk factor for urinary incontinence due to a shorter urethra and the effects of pregnancy and childbirth on pelvic floor support.
###
Which of the following is NOT a risk factor specifically for stress urinary incontinence?
A) Multiple vaginal deliveries
B) Obesity
C) Chronic cough/smoking
D) Nulliparity
Correct Answer: D) Nulliparity
Explanation: Vaginal childbirth weakens pelvic floor support and increases SUI risk, whereas nulliparous women have a comparatively lower risk.
###
Which class of medication should be avoided when treating stress urinary incontinence in a male patient?
A) Anticholinergic drugs
B) Alpha-agonists (e.g., pseudoephedrine)
C) Pelvic floor physiotherapy
D) Artificial urinary sphincter placement
Correct Answer: A) Anticholinergic drugs
Explanation: Anticholinergics target detrusor overactivity in urge incontinence and have no role in stress incontinence, which in men is usually secondary to sphincter injury after prostate surgery.
###
What is the most common overall indication for placement of an indwelling Foley catheter?
A) Obtaining a sterile urine sample
B) Drainage of acute or chronic urinary retention
C) Monitoring hourly urine output
D) Intravesical instillation of chemotherapy
Correct Answer: B) Drainage of acute or chronic urinary retention
Explanation: Drainage of acute or chronic retention (or post-void residual) is the most common indication for catheterization; monitoring urine output is the second most common.
###
A patient started on an antimuscarinic for urge incontinence develops several adverse effects. Which of the following is NOT typically caused by anticholinergic medications?
A) Diarrhea
B) Dry mouth
C) Urinary retention
D) Confusion
Correct Answer: A) Diarrhea
Explanation: Anticholinergics cause constipation, not diarrhea, along with dry mouth, blurred vision, urinary retention, and confusion.
###
Before initiating mirabegron for urge incontinence, which of the following should prompt the greatest caution or avoidance of therapy?
A) Mild constipation
B) History of well-controlled hypertension
C) Severe, uncontrolled hypertension
D) Age over 65 years
Correct Answer: C) Severe, uncontrolled hypertension
Explanation: Mirabegron can raise blood pressure, so severe uncontrolled hypertension is a major safety concern, whereas significant urinary retention with high PVR is a relative caution rather than the primary contraindication.
###
During cystometry, a patient's intravesical pressure (Pves) is 45 cmH2O and abdominal pressure (Pabd) is 15 cmH2O. What is the calculated detrusor pressure (Pdet)?
A) 60 cmH2O
B) 15 cmH2O
C) 45 cmH2O
D) 30 cmH2O
Correct Answer: D) 30 cmH2O
Explanation: Detrusor pressure is calculated as Pdet = Pves − Pabd, so 45 − 15 = 30 cmH2O.
###
A 6-year-old child is undergoing urodynamic evaluation. Using the standard pediatric formula, what is the expected bladder capacity?
A) 120 mL
B) 240 mL
C) 180 mL
D) 300 mL
Correct Answer: B) 240 mL
Explanation: Pediatric bladder capacity is estimated as (Age + 2) × 30 mL; for a 6-year-old this is (6+2) × 30 = 240 mL.
###
Before initiating mirabegron for the treatment of urge urinary incontinence, which of the following conditions is the most appropriate reason to avoid or use the drug with significant caution?
A) Dysuria
B) Significant urinary retention with high post-void residual
C) Kidney stones
D) Hematuria
Correct Answer: B) Significant urinary retention with high post-void residual
Explanation: Mirabegron (a β3-adrenergic agonist) relaxes the detrusor muscle during bladder filling, which may worsen pre-existing urinary retention. Therefore, patients with significant urinary retention and a high post-void residual should generally avoid the drug or receive it only with careful monitoring. Dysuria, kidney stones, and hematuria are not contraindications by themselves.
