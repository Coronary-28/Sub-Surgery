During TURP, a patient develops confusion, nausea, bradycardia, and hypertension near the end of a prolonged resection. Serum sodium is low. This is best explained by:
A) Absorption of hypotonic irrigating fluid causing dilutional hyponatremia
B) Sepsis from catheter-associated infection
C) Acute blood loss anemia
D) Anaphylaxis to anesthetic agent
E) Pre-existing chronic kidney disease
Correct Answer: A) Absorption of hypotonic irrigating fluid causing dilutional hyponatremia
Explanation: TUR syndrome results from absorption of hypotonic irrigating solution through open venous sinuses during resection, causing hypervolemic hyponatremia with confusion, bradycardia, hypertension, and visual disturbances.
### 
Which factor is most associated with increased risk of TUR syndrome?
A) Short resection time
B) Use of bipolar resection with saline irrigation
C) Resection time greater than 90 minutes
D) Young patient age
E) Use of isotonic irrigation fluid
Correct Answer: C) Resection time greater than 90 minutes
Explanation: TUR syndrome risk increases with resection times exceeding 90 minutes and is more common in older men; bipolar TURP performed under saline (isotonic) irrigation reduces this risk.
### 
A patient requires long-term indwelling catheterization. Compared to latex, silicone catheters are preferred because they:
A) Are cheaper
B) Have a larger balloon capacity
C) Are only available in smaller sizes
D) Induce less tissue inflammation
E) Require less lubrication
Correct Answer: D) Induce less tissue inflammation
Explanation: Silicone catheters induce less tissue inflammation than latex, making them preferable for long-term catheterization, despite latex being cheaper and more widely available.
### 
A 24 French Foley catheter has an outer diameter of approximately:
A) 24 mm
B) 3 mm
C) 8 mm
D) 12 mm
E) 1 cm exactly
Correct Answer: C) 8 mm
Explanation: Catheter French size divided by 3 gives the outer diameter in millimeters; 24 ÷ 3 = 8 mm.
### 
Which of the following is a recognized indication for urethral catheterization?
A) Monitoring urine output in a critically ill patient
B) Suspected urethral injury with blood at the meatus
C) High-riding prostate on rectal exam after trauma
D) Acute prostatitis with no retention
E) Pelvic fracture with suspected urethral disruption
Correct Answer: A) Monitoring urine output in a critically ill patient
Explanation: Monitoring urine output is a standard, common indication for catheterization. Suspected urethral injury (blood at meatus, high-riding prostate, pelvic fracture with urethral disruption) is a contraindication until imaging excludes injury.
### 
Which of the following is a contraindication to transurethral Foley catheter placement?
A) Acute urinary retention from BPH
B) Suspected urethral injury
C) Postoperative monitoring
D) Need for a clean urine sample
E) Intravesical BCG instillation
Correct Answer: B) Suspected urethral injury
Explanation: Suspected urethral injury is a contraindication to urethral catheterization; a retrograde urethrogram should be performed first, and a suprapubic catheter used if drainage is needed.
### 
Chronic urinary retention is best distinguished from acute retention by which feature?
A) Sudden onset of severe suprapubic pain
B) Painless, palpable, and percussible bladder
C) Fever and systemic toxicity
D) Gross hematuria
E) Rapid resolution with a single catheterization
Correct Answer: B) Painless, palpable, and percussible bladder
Explanation: Chronic retention classically presents as a painless, palpable/percussible bladder due to gradual distension, in contrast to the painful presentation of acute retention.
### 
A 60-year-old man with ischemic heart disease presents with fever of 40°C, HR 125, BP 120/65, and suprapubic tenderness consistent with acute bacterial prostatitis with urinary retention. The most appropriate management is:
A) Transurethral catheterization, IV antibiotics, and admission
B) Oral antibiotics and outpatient follow-up
C) Prostatic massage to obtain culture, then antibiotics
D) Suprapubic catheterization, IV antibiotics, and admission
E) Immediate transurethral resection of the prostate
Correct Answer: D) Suprapubic catheterization, IV antibiotics, and admission
Explanation: In acute bacterial prostatitis, urethral catheterization and instrumentation are contraindicated due to risk of bacteremia; urinary retention should be managed with a suprapubic catheter along with IV antibiotics and admission.
### 
Which of the following clinical situations requires urgent decompression of the urinary tract?
A) Bilateral non-obstructing renal stones
B) A small asymptomatic stone with mild pain
C) An obstructed, infected collecting system
D) Simple physiological hydronephrosis of pregnancy
E) Chronic painless bladder distension
Correct Answer: C) An obstructed, infected collecting system
Explanation: An obstructed and infected urinary system (obstructive pyelonephritis/pyonephrosis) is a urologic emergency requiring urgent decompression via JJ stent or nephrostomy to prevent sepsis and renal destruction.
### 
Which of the following is NOT a recognized complication of long-term double-J (JJ) ureteral stenting?
A) Infection
B) Urothelial cancer
C) Flank pain during urination (reflux)
D) Hematuria
Correct Answer: B) Urothelial cancer
Explanation: Long-standing catheters (not JJ stents specifically) are linked to stone formation and bladder cancer risk with prolonged indwelling urethral catheters; urothelial cancer is not a typical direct complication attributed to JJ stents, whose common complications are infection, hematuria, and irritative/positional flank pain.
### 
Which of the following is NOT a side effect of anticholinergic (antimuscarinic) medications used for overactive bladder?
A) Dry mouth
B) Constipation
C) Confusion
D) Diarrhea
Correct Answer: D) Diarrhea
Explanation: Anticholinergics cause constipation, not diarrhea, along with dry mouth, blurred vision, and confusion, due to inhibition of parasympathetic (muscarinic) activity on the gut and other organs.
### 
A fetus is found to have antenatal hydronephrosis on prenatal ultrasound. Appropriate postnatal evaluation includes:
A) Renal ultrasound with VCUG and/or renal scan depending on severity
B) CT scan only
C) Cystoscopy only
D) Brain MRI
Correct Answer: A) Renal ultrasound with VCUG and/or renal scan depending on severity
Explanation: Postnatal workup of antenatal hydronephrosis is guided by severity, using renal ultrasound as the initial test, followed by VCUG (to assess for reflux) and/or renal scan (to assess function/obstruction) as indicated.
### 
A pregnant woman is found to have mild hydronephrosis on ultrasound with no symptoms of infection or stone disease. This is most likely explained by:
A) An occult ureteral stone
B) Bladder tumor
C) Compression of the ureter by the gravid uterus (physiological hydronephrosis)
D) Undiagnosed BPH
Correct Answer: C) Compression of the ureter by the gravid uterus (physiological hydronephrosis)
Explanation: Mild hydronephrosis in pregnancy, particularly on the right side, is commonly physiological due to compression of the ureter by the enlarging gravid uterus and does not necessarily indicate pathology.
