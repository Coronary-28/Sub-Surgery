BPH most commonly arises from which zone of the prostate?
A) Transitional zone
B) Peripheral zone
C) Central zone
D) Anterior fibromuscular stroma
E) Capsular zone
Correct Answer: A) Transitional zone
Explanation: BPH develops in the transitional zone, whereas prostate cancer arises predominantly (70–80%) from the peripheral zone.

###
In uncomplicated bladder outflow obstruction due to BPH, which finding is typical?
A) Rapid symptomatic improvement with finasteride
B) Symptom relief with alpha-1 adrenoceptor agonists
C) Reduced voiding pressure
D) Voiding flow rate less than 15 mL/sec
E) Prostatectomy as first-line treatment
Correct Answer: D) Voiding flow rate less than 15 mL/sec
Explanation: A maximum flow rate below 15 mL/sec (with <10 mL/sec strongly suggestive) is typical of obstruction. Finasteride takes 6+ months to work, and alpha-blockers (not agonists) relieve symptoms.

###
Which of the following is NOT a symptom of the voiding (obstructive) phase in BPH?
A) Weak stream
B) Frequency
C) Hesitancy
D) Straining
E) Terminal dribbling
Correct Answer: B) Frequency
Explanation: Frequency is a storage symptom. Voiding/obstructive symptoms include hesitancy, weak stream, straining, intermittency, and terminal dribbling.
###
Which symptom is NOT related to urine storage phase dysfunction?
A) Nocturia
B) Urgency
C) Weak stream
D) Frequency
Correct Answer: C) Weak stream
Explanation: Weak stream is a voiding (obstructive) phase symptom. Storage symptoms include frequency, urgency, nocturia, and urge incontinence.
###
A 65-year-old man with BPH has an IPSS of 22 (severe). What is the best initial treatment?
A) Transurethral microwave therapy
B) Observation alone
C) Terazosin
D) Immediate TURP
E) Finasteride monotherapy for immediate relief
Correct Answer: C) Terazosin
Explanation: Alpha-1 blockers such as terazosin are first-line medical therapy for BPH, even with severe IPSS scores. Surgery is reserved for failed medical therapy or complications.
###
Which of the following is NOT used in the treatment of BPH?
A) Alpha-1 blocker
B) 5-alpha reductase inhibitor
C) Beta-blocker
D) Alpha-1 agonist
E) Antimuscarinic
Correct Answer: D) Alpha-1 agonist
Explanation: Alpha-1 agonists cause smooth muscle contraction and would worsen bladder outflow obstruction. Beta-blockers also have no established role in BPH, but alpha-agonists are the classically incorrect option since they directly oppose the mechanism of alpha-blocker therapy.
###
Tamsulosin, used in the treatment of BPH, acts as a(n):
A) Alpha-1 agonist
B) Alpha-2 agonist
C) Alpha-1 antagonist
D) Beta-2 agonist
E) Non-selective alpha antagonist
Correct Answer: C) Alpha-1 antagonist
Explanation: Tamsulosin is a selective alpha-1A antagonist that relaxes prostatic and bladder neck smooth muscle to improve urinary flow.
###
Which of the following is an indication for open (simple) prostatectomy rather than TURP?
A) Prostate size greater than 80 grams
B) Recurrent UTI
C) Mild IPSS symptoms
D) PVR less than 50 mL
E) First-line medical therapy failure with small prostate
Correct Answer: A) Prostate size greater than 80 grams
Explanation: A prostate weighing more than 80 g is generally too large for safe transurethral resection (increased TUR syndrome risk), making open (or laparoscopic/robotic) simple prostatectomy preferable.
###
Which statement about anticholinergic drugs in BPH is FALSE?
A) They can treat storage (OAB) symptoms
B) They are indicated when post-void residual is greater than 150 mL
C) They are used in combination with alpha-blockers
D) They may cause dry mouth and constipation
Correct Answer: B) They are indicated when post-void residual is greater than 150 mL
Explanation: Anticholinergics are contraindicated, not indicated, when PVR exceeds 150 mL, due to the risk of precipitating acute urinary retention.
###
Antimuscarinic therapy for LUTS/BPH is contraindicated:
A) Only in men with large and persistent post-void residual volumes
B) In all men with OAB symptoms
C) In all men with BPH regardless of PVR
D) In all men with an enlarged prostate
E) Only when combined with alpha-blockers
Correct Answer: A) Only in men with large and persistent post-void residual volumes
Explanation: Antimuscarinics are contraindicated specifically in men with large, persistent PVR due to retention risk; they can safely be combined with alpha-blockers for mixed storage and voiding symptoms.
###
Which of the following is NOT a side effect of anticholinergic medications?
A) Dry mouth
B) Diarrhea
C) Urinary retention/constipation
D) Confusion
Correct Answer: B) Diarrhea
Explanation: Anticholinergics cause constipation (not diarrhea), along with dry mouth, urinary retention, blurred vision, and confusion.
###
Which of the following is NOT a recognized side effect of tamsulosin?
A) Retrograde ejaculation
B) Postural hypotension
C) Rhinitis
D) Decreased libido
Correct Answer: D) Decreased libido
Explanation: Tamsulosin's known side effects are retrograde ejaculation (most common), postural hypotension, and rhinitis/nasal congestion; decreased libido is not a typical alpha-blocker side effect.
###
Regarding post-void residual (PVR) assessment in BPH, which statement is most accurate?
A) PVR is best assessed by bladder ultrasound, and assessment is important before surgery for related conditions or in patients with voiding symptoms
B) PVR should always be measured after every single void with volumes over 300 mL
C) PVR can only be measured by catheterization
D) PVR has no clinical relevance in BPH management
Correct Answer: A) PVR is best assessed by bladder ultrasound, and assessment is important before surgery for related conditions or in patients with voiding symptoms
Explanation: PVR is typically measured using ultrasound (avoiding invasive catheterization) and should be assessed in patients with significant voiding symptoms or before certain interventions, though it is not measured after every single void.
###
Which symptom is LEAST specific for bladder outflow obstruction?
A) Nocturia
B) Intermittency
C) Hesitancy
D) Reduced urinary stream
E) Terminal dribbling
Correct Answer: A) Nocturia
Explanation: Nocturia can result from many causes (polyuria, cardiac disease, aging) besides bladder outflow obstruction, making it the least specific symptom among the choices.
###
Which of the following is NOT a storage symptom of BPH?
A) Frequency
B) Urgency
C) Nocturia
D) Dysuria
Correct Answer: D) Dysuria
Explanation: Dysuria reflects pain during urination (typically from inflammation) and is not a standard storage LUTS; storage symptoms include frequency, urgency, nocturia, and urge incontinence.
###
Which of the following does NOT occur during the voiding phase?
A) Hesitancy
B) Nocturia
C) Weak stream
Correct Answer: B) Nocturia
Explanation: Nocturia is a storage-phase symptom, while hesitancy and weak stream are voiding-phase symptoms.
###
A 65-year-old man with LUTS has IPSS 22 and PVR 50 mL. The most appropriate next step is:
A) TURP
B) Watchful waiting
C) Tamsulosin
Correct Answer: C) Tamsulosin
Explanation: With a severe IPSS but only mildly elevated PVR and no indication for surgery, first-line medical therapy with an alpha-blocker (tamsulosin) is appropriate.
###
A 65-year-old man with BPH has IPSS symptom score of 22, PVR 50 cc, prostate volume 60 cc, severe detrusor instability on cystometrogram, and high-grade obstruction on pressure-flow study. The best next step to improve symptoms is:
A) Oxybutynin
B) TURP
C) Tamsulosin
D) Laser prostatectomy
E) Finasteride
Correct Answer: C) Tamsulosin
Explanation: Despite urodynamic evidence of obstruction and detrusor overactivity, initial management remains medical therapy with an alpha-blocker before considering surgery, given no absolute surgical indication is present.
###
A 65-year-old man on tamsulosin and a 5-alpha reductase inhibitor for 2 months continues to have obstructive symptoms. The next step is:
A) Change medication immediately
B) Continue current therapy and reassess later
C) Proceed to TURP
Correct Answer: B) Continue current therapy and reassess later
Explanation: 5-alpha reductase inhibitors require at least 6 months to show clinical benefit; persistent symptoms after only 3 months, without complications, warrant continued therapy and reassessment rather than surgery.
###
Tamsulosin combined with a PDE5 inhibitor should be used with caution due to the risk of:
A) Hyperglycemia
B) Renal failure
C) Infection
D) Hypotension
Correct Answer: D) Hypotension
Explanation: Both alpha-blockers and PDE5 inhibitors cause vasodilation; their combination increases the risk of significant hypotension.
