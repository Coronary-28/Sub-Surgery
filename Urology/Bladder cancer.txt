Which bladder tumor originates from the urachus?
A) Transitional cell carcinoma
B) Sarcoma
C) Squamous cell carcinoma
D) Adenocarcinoma
E) Small cell carcinoma
Correct Answer: D) Adenocarcinoma
Explanation: Urachal remnants at the bladder dome give rise to adenocarcinoma, often presenting with mucusuria. TCC and SCC arise from urothelium and chronic irritation respectively, not urachal remnants.
###
Which type of bladder cancer is caused due to chronic irritation?
A) Transitional cell carcinoma
B) Adenocarcinoma
C) Squamous cell carcinoma
D) Sarcoma
E) Small cell carcinoma
Correct Answer: C) Squamous cell carcinoma
Explanation: Chronic irritation (Schistosoma, long-term catheterization, bladder stones, recurrent UTI) causes squamous metaplasia of the urothelium, progressing to SCC over time.
###
Which of the following environmental factors is highly associated with bladder cancer?
A) Smoking
B) Irradiation
C) Occupation
D) Age
E) Diet
Correct Answer: A) Smoking
Explanation: Smoking accounts for roughly half of bladder cancers, conferring a 2–6× increased risk via carcinogenic arylamines. Occupational and radiation exposure are recognized risk factors but contribute far less overall.
###
Typical presentation of bladder cancer:
A) Painful urination
B) Dysuria
C) Painless intermittent gross hematuria
D) Urinary frequency
E) Urgency
Correct Answer: C) Painless intermittent gross hematuria
Explanation: This is the classic presentation, seen in 80–85% of patients, and represents total hematuria from the bladder. Irritative symptoms like frequency or dysuria are less common and suggest CIS.
###
68-year-old male patient diagnosed with bladder TCC; on TURBT histology came back as T2 bladder TCC. Best next treatment option is:i
A) Radical cystoprostatectomy
B) Intravesical immunotherapy
C) Intravenous chemotherapy and TURBT
D) Re-do TURBT in 3 months
E) Intravesical chemotherapy
Correct Answer: A) Radical cystoprostatectomy
Explanation: T2 disease invades the muscularis propria, defining muscle-invasive bladder cancer, which requires radical cystectomy (± neoadjuvant cisplatin-based chemotherapy). Intravesical therapies are reserved for non-muscle-invasive disease.
###
Which is considered the best for follow-up of bladder cancer after resection?
A) Cystoscopy
B) Ultrasound
C) CT urography
D) IVU
E) Cystometry
Correct Answer: A) Cystoscopy
Explanation: Cystoscopy is the gold standard for surveillance, allowing direct visualization and biopsy of recurrent lesions. Imaging modalities lack the sensitivity to detect small mucosal recurrences.
###
Which one of the following intravesical treatments CANNOT be given immediately after bladder cancer resection?
A) Mitomycin
B) BCG
C) Thiotepa
D) Gemcitabine
E) Doxorubicin
Correct Answer: B) BCG
Explanation: Intravesical chemotherapy (such as Mitomycin) can be administered as a single dose within 24 hours of resection to eliminate circulating tumor cells. However, intravesical BCG (an attenuated strain of mycobacteria) must be delayed for at least 2 weeks post-resection. This delay allows the resection site to heal completely, preventing potentially fatal systemic mycobacterial dissemination and BCG-sepsis.
###
High-grade bladder tumor characteristics:
A) High progression AND high recurrence
B) High progression AND low recurrence
C) Low progression AND low recurrence
D) Low progression AND high recurrence
E) No recurrence and no progression
Correct Answer: A) High progression AND high recurrence
Explanation: High-grade tumors carry both high recurrence and high progression risk, whereas low-grade tumors have high recurrence but low progression potential.
###
A 62-year-old male presents with intermittent, painless gross hematuria. Cystoscopy reveals a papillary bladder tumor, and he undergoes a complete transurethral resection of bladder tumor (TURBT). The final histopathology report confirms a Ta, low-grade non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC).Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in the immediate management of this patient?
A) Intravesical BCG
B) Intravesical Mitomycin C
C) Radical cystectomy
D) Observation only
E) Partial cystectomy
Correct Answer: B) Intravesical Mitomycin C
Explanation: A single immediate instillation post-TURBT reduces recurrence by ~50% in Ta low-grade tumors. BCG is reserved for T1, CIS, or high-grade Ta disease due to its higher toxicity.
###
Egyptian man with previous Schistosoma parasite infection — most common bladder cancer type:
A) Transitional cell carcinoma
B) Adenocarcinoma
C) Squamous cell carcinoma
D) Sarcoma
E) Small cell carcinoma
Correct Answer: C) Squamous cell carcinoma
Explanation: S. haematobium is endemic in Egypt; eggs deposited in the bladder wall trigger chronic inflammation and squamous metaplasia, leading to SCC rather than TCC.
###
Risk factor for bladder cancer from occupation:
A) Obesity
B) Working in rubber/dye/textile/paint industry
C) Hypertension
D) Diabetes mellitus
E) Physical inactivity
Correct Answer: B) Working in rubber/dye/textile/paint industry
Explanation: Aromatic amines such as benzidine and beta-naphthylamine are established occupational carcinogens in these industries, as well as in painting and hairdressing.
###
What is the definitive treatment for muscle-invasive bladder cancer?
A) Chemotherapy alone
B) Cystoscopy + biopsy
C) Partial cystectomy
D) Radical cystectomy (+ ileal conduit/neobladder)
E) Intravesical BCG
Correct Answer: D) Radical cystectomy (+ ileal conduit/neobladder)
Explanation: Radical cystectomy with urinary diversion is the gold standard for T2+ disease. Chemotherapy alone and intravesical therapy are inadequate once muscularis propria is invaded.
###
Which group is more affected by bladder cancer?
A) Women
B) Children
C) Older males (especially smokers)
D) Diabetics
E) Young adults
Correct Answer: C) Older males (especially smokers)
Explanation: Male:female ratio is roughly 3:1, with peak incidence at 60–70 years; smoking further amplifies this predominant demographic risk.
###
Which of the following is a risk factor for bladder cancer?
A) Obesity
B) Working in rubber industry
C) Hypertension
D) Family history of bladder cancer
E) Diabetes mellitus
Correct Answer: B) Working in rubber industry
Explanation: Occupational exposure to aromatic amines in the rubber industry is a well-established, strong risk factor. Family history confers only a modest (two-fold) increase in comparison.
###
Patient with uncontrolled HTN, colon cancer treated with cyclophosphamide + radiotherapy, now has invasive bladder cancer — what is LEAST likely to increase bladder cancer risk?
A) Smoking
B) Radiotherapy
C) Cyclophosphamide chemotherapy
D) Poor control of hypertension
E) Pelvic irradiation
Correct Answer: D) Poor control of hypertension
Explanation: Hypertension is not a recognized bladder cancer risk factor, whereas cyclophosphamide, pelvic radiotherapy, and smoking are all established causative agents.
###
Which of the following is a common risk factor for squamous cell carcinoma of the bladder?
A) Staphylococcus aureus
B) Schistosoma haematobium
C) Escherichia coli
D) Proteus mirabilis
E) All of the above
Correct Answer: B) Schistosoma haematobium
Explanation: Eggs lodging in the bladder wall provoke chronic granulomatous inflammation and squamous metaplasia, culminating in SCC — unlike ordinary bacterial UTIs.
###
60-year-old smoker with painless intermittent gross hematuria. Most likely diagnosis?
A) TCC (transitional cell carcinoma) bladder cancer
B) Adenocarcinoma
C) Small cell carcinoma
D) Squamous cell carcinoma
E) Sarcoma
Correct Answer: A) TCC (transitional cell carcinoma) bladder cancer
Explanation: TCC accounts for ~90% of bladder cancers and is strongly linked to smoking, presenting classically with painless intermittent gross hematuria.
###
Which of the following is NOT a risk factor for bladder cancer?
A) Smoking
B) Aromatic amine exposure
C) Occupational exposure to paint
D) Male gender
E) Alcohol
Correct Answer: E) Alcohol
Explanation: Alcohol has no established causal link to bladder cancer, unlike smoking, occupational carcinogen exposure, and male gender, which are all recognized risk factors.
###
Most common cancer associated with persistent urachus:
A) TCC
B) Adenocarcinoma
C) Squamous cell carcinoma
D) Sarcoma
E) Small cell carcinoma
Correct Answer: B) Adenocarcinoma
Explanation: A persistent urachal remnant at the bladder dome predisposes to adenocarcinoma, distinct from the urothelium-derived TCC affecting the rest of the bladder.
###
Patient with bladder cancer stage T1, high risk for progression, underwent resection; after 2 weeks what is the next step?
A) Cystoscopy with intravesical BCG

D) Observation
E) Repeat TURBT
Correct Answer: E) Repeat TURBT
Explanation: High-risk T1 tumors require re-resection (repeat TURBT) to exclude understaging or residual disease before committing to intravesical therapy.

###
Urine cytology is most sensitive for?
A) Low-grade bladder tumors
B) High-grade bladder cancer and carcinoma in situ (CIS)
C) Renal stones
D) BPH
E) Ureteric strictures
Correct Answer: B) High-grade bladder cancer and carcinoma in situ (CIS)
Explanation: Urine cytology detects poorly differentiated cells shed from high-grade tumors and CIS with high sensitivity, but often misses well-differentiated low-grade lesions.
###
Which of the following is NOT a component of trimodal therapy for bladder-preserving treatment of muscle-invasive bladder cancer?
A) Maximal TURBT
B) Concurrent chemotherapy
C) Radical cystectomy
D) Radiotherapy
Correct Answer: C) Radical cystectomy
Explanation: Trimodal therapy combines maximal TURBT with concurrent chemotherapy and radiotherapy specifically to spare the bladder; radical cystectomy is the alternative it aims to avoid.
