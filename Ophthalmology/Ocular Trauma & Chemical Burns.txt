A 24-year-old man presents after blunt trauma to his right eye. He complains of vertical diplopia, especially on looking up. CT scan shows a right orbital floor fracture with entrapment of the inferior rectus muscle. Which of the following statements is FALSE?
A) Diplopia is worse on upgaze
B) Entrapment of the inferior rectus limits elevation of the affected eye
C) Hypoaesthesia over the cheek may occur due to infraorbital nerve injury
D) The squint decreases when the patient looks upward
E) CT scan is the investigation of choice
Correct Answer: D) The squint decreases when the patient looks upward
Explanation: Entrapment classically limits elevation, so the diplopia/squint worsens (not decreases) on upgaze. A, B, C, and E are all accurate — infraorbital nerve runs through the floor, and CT with coronal cuts is the imaging of choice.

Commotio retinae is seen in:
A) Central retinal artery occlusion
B) Papilloedema
C) Central retinal vein occlusion
D) Concussion injury
Correct Answer: D) Concussion injury
Explanation: According to Ophthalmology Book, commotio retinae is typically observed following a closed eye injury, or concussion injury. In such cases, areas of retinal haemorrhage may be seen alongside the characteristic patches of white, retinal oedema known as commotio retinae (Berlin's oedema). This mechanical disruption of the photoreceptor outer segments is caused by blunt contusion and is entirely unrelated to papilloedema or retinal vascular occlusions.

~Choose the correct answers~
Select all correct statements regarding hyphaema:
A) Is a fluid collection of white cells in the anterior chamber
B) May be associated with a low intraocular pressure
C) Is treated with restriction of activity
D) If it recurs within a short time, may result in more severe problems
E) Is treated with steroid drops
Correct Answer: C) Is treated with restriction of activity / D) If it recurs within a short time, may result in more severe problems / E) Is treated with steroid drops
Explanation: A is false — hyphema is blood, not white cells (white cells = hypopyon). B is false — hyphema is associated with raised IOP (blood blocks the trabecular meshwork), not low IOP. C is true — rest/activity restriction is standard management. D is true — re-bleeding (typically day 2–5) is often worse than the original bleed with higher complication risk. E is true — These will help to reduce any associated inflammation and reduce the incidence of rebleeds.

A patient presents with a hazy cornea following an alkali burn. What is the diagnosis, and what is the immediate first step in management? [img: hazycornea.png ]
A) Uveitis; cycloplegics
B) Chemical eye burn; topical steroids
C) Bacterial keratitis; topical antibiotics
D) Corneal ulcer; corneal scrape
E) Chemical eye burn; irrigation
Correct Answer: E) Chemical eye burn; irrigation
Explanation: Irrigation must begin immediately, before history or examination. Once the eye is irrigated and pH neutral, ongoing treatment includes topical antibiotics, cautious steroids, and oral/topical vitamin C plus tetracyclines to support healing and prevent corneal melting.

A 48-year-old mechanic was using a pneumatic pump to lift a vehicle when a pipe burst, blowing air at 150 psi onto his left eye. He now has blurred vision and pain. On slit-lamp examination, the inferior half of the anterior chamber appears red, and superiorly the pupil is irregular. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A) Subconjunctival haemorrhage
B) Acute angle-closure glaucoma
C) Vitreous haemorrhage
D) Central retinal artery occlusion
E) Hyphema with traumatic iris sphincter tear
Correct Answer: E) Hyphema with traumatic iris sphincter tear
Explanation: Blood layering inferiorly in the anterior chamber is hyphema from torn iris/ciliary vessels, and an irregular pupil after blunt trauma reflects traumatic mydriasis/iris sphincter tears — both classic findings after a high-pressure blunt blast injury to the eye.

A 56-year-old man develops a sudden, bright red patch on the white of his left eye. He has had a chest infection recently. The eye is slightly sore but his vision is unaffected. What is shown, and what is the appropriate management? [img: subconjunctivalhaemorrhagechestinfection.png ]
A) Hyphema; bed rest and shield
B) Scleritis; systemic work-up needed
C) Chemical burn; irrigation
D) Corneal ulcer; antibiotics
E) Subconjunctival haemorrhage; reassurance, usually self-limiting
Correct Answer: E) Subconjunctival haemorrhage; reassurance, usually self-limiting
Explanation: Recent chest infection is a clue toward vigorous coughing, which raises intrathoracic and venous pressure, transmitted back to the delicate conjunctival capillaries and causing one to rupture. This produces a benign, self-limiting subconjunctival haemorrhage — alarming in appearance but not sight-threatening, resolving over 1–2 weeks without treatment.

After an orbital floor fracture, a patient is unable to look up or down. What is the most likely cause?
A) Optic neuropathy
B) Lateral rectus palsy
C) Inferior rectus entrapment
D) Orbital cellulitis
Correct Answer: C) Inferior rectus entrapment
Explanation: The inferior rectus is classically entrapped in an orbital floor fracture; a physically tethered muscle can restrict movement in both directions, confirmed with a positive forced duction test.
###
A patient presents with a history of blunt ocular trauma. Which secondary condition is this patient at a lifelong, delayed risk of developing, necessitating permanent monitoring?
A) Retinitis
B) Glaucoma
C) Cataract
D) Dry eye
Correct Answer: B) Glaucoma
Explanation: Blunt ocular trauma can cause angle recession, which is a tear within the ciliary body face at the drainage angle. While often initially asymptomatic, this structural damage carries a lifelong risk of delayed-onset glaucoma (sometimes appearing years after the injury), which requires permanent intraocular pressure (IOP) monitoring. Although traumatic cataract is also a recognized complication of blunt trauma, the specific mention of "delayed/lifelong risk" classically identifies secondary glaucoma as the intended answer.
###
Best initial treatment for traumatic hyphema:
A) Surgery
B) Observation only
C) Bed rest and cycloplegics with head elevation
D) Aspirin
Correct Answer: C) Bed rest and cycloplegics with head elevation
Explanation: Initial management is rest with the head elevated, a protective shield, and cycloplegics, treating any IOP rise; aspirin/NSAIDs must be avoided as they worsen bleeding. Surgical washout is reserved only for non-clearing hyphema or uncontrolled IOP.
###
For a suspected penetrating (open-globe) eye injury, what is the first step in management?
A) Apply a pressure pad
B) Check intraocular pressure
C) Protect the eye with a rigid shield
D) Irrigate the eye
Correct Answer: C) Protect the eye with a rigid shield
Explanation: A rigid protective shield (never a pressure pad) should be placed immediately, alongside stopping further examination, keeping the patient fasted, and urgent referral.
###
After ocular trauma, a peaked or teardrop-shaped pupil suggests what diagnosis?
A) Globe perforation (open globe)
B) Hyphema
C) Acute angle-closure glaucoma
D) Horner syndrome
Correct Answer: A) Globe perforation (open globe)
Explanation: A peaked, teardrop, or irregular pupil points toward the site of a leak/uveal prolapse and is a red-flag sign of open globe injury.
###
Penetrating eye trauma most commonly causes:
A) Conjunctival haemorrhage
B) Iris prolapse and pupil distortion
C) Subconjunctival haemorrhage
D) Hypopyon
Correct Answer: B) Iris prolapse and pupil distortion
Explanation: Visible uveal tissue (iris/ciliary body) prolapsing through the wound, with resulting pupil distortion, is a hallmark open-globe red flag requiring immediate shield and urgent surgical referral.
